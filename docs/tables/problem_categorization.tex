    \vspace{0.5em}
    \begin{flushleft}
    \scriptsize
    $^{a}$~\texttt{Knapsack.py} uses $c_i = 5\cdot[(B x_i/\sqrt{p}+3)/3.5]^{\mathrm{poly\_deg}}(1+\eta_i)$ with
    $\mathrm{poly\_deg}=7$, $p=5$, multiplicative uniform noise of width $0.5$, and a fixed
    $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(0.5)$ projection $B$.\\[2pt]
    $^{b}$~``Well-specified'' means the hypothesis class can represent the conditional mean.
    Irreducible noise still floors regret; the observed oracle ceiling is $\approx 0.052$
    (see \texttt{memory/oracle\_ceiling.md}).\\[2pt]
    $^{c}$~\texttt{budgetalloc} is the clearest \emph{objective} mis-spec: $y$ itself is predictable,
    but the CO objective $1 - \prod_j (1 - z_j y_{ij})$ is non-linear in $y$, so prediction errors
    compound non-linearly in the decision. Per \citet{elmachtoub2025misspec} this is the regime where
    decision-aware surrogates most clearly beat MSE; observed gap is $\sim 11\times$ in our Phase-2 sweep.\\[2pt]
    $^{d}$~\texttt{sp\_synth}/\texttt{sp\_planted} form a deliberate well/mis contrast: same 5$\times$5
    shortest-path problem, same \emph{linear} prediction head (\texttt{-{}-n\_layers 1}), but the true
    $c(x)$ is polynomial of degree 6 (mis-spec) versus linear (well-spec).\\[2pt]
    \textit{Columns.} \textbf{DGP}: the true $c=f(x)$ in data-generation code; ``Unknown'' for real data.
    \textbf{$\partial_c$~lin.}: whether the CO objective is linear in the predicted coefficients.
    \textbf{Specification bin}: our categorisation — \emph{well-specified}, \emph{mis-specified}
    (by hypothesis class or by objective), \emph{small-data}, or \emph{real / unknown}.
    \textbf{Expected}: hypothesis \emph{going in}, not the observed outcome; the chapter compares expected
    versus observed.
    \end{flushleft}
